# CDIO alignment

Status: public framework verified; local mappings and adoption choices require programme review. Source CDIO-01/02 in [references](REFERENCES.md).

CDIO describes an engineering education approach grounded in conceiving, designing, implementing and operating systems, with disciplinary knowledge and personal/interpersonal skills. It does not prescribe a universal mark distribution or require every course to teach all four lifecycle stages.

| Standards 3.0 focus | PMS design implication (proposed) |
|---|---|
| 1 context; 2 outcomes; 3 curriculum | Record approved programme context, competencies, course mapping and progressive development |
| 4 introduction; 5 design-implement experiences; 6 workspaces | Trace authentic design/build experiences, project artifacts and learning resources |
| 7 integrated experiences; 8 active learning | Record teaching strategies, activities and observable evidence |
| 9 faculty competence; 10 teaching competence | Trace lecturer development and evidence with authorized access |
| 11 learning assessment | Align appropriate instruments to individual/team competencies |
| 12 programme evaluation | Evidence-based review, actions and follow-up |

Use CDIO Syllabus 3.0 as a reference for competency design; store the approved DSE adaptation separately from external identifiers. Teach/use/assess or introduced/reinforced/mastered labels are local mapping choices and must not be conflated. A mapping is not proof of attainment. For group projects, retain contribution and individually attributable assessment evidence where required. For field visits, participation and reflection are supporting evidence unless a valid assessment explicitly measures an outcome.

Test mapping scope/version, evidence attribution, access control and historical integrity. Do not claim CDIO compliance or certification merely because PMS has a feature.
